Summarize the following notes in three bullet points. Notes: The town council met on Tuesday to discuss the new bike lanes planned for Main Street. Several shop owners worried that removing parking spaces would hurt business, while cyclists argued that safer streets would bring more customers on foot and by bike. The traffic engineer presented data from three nearby towns that added similar lanes over the past five years; in two of them retail sales rose slightly and in one they stayed flat. The council also heard about the cost: about 1.2 million dollars, mostly covered by a state grant that expires next spring if not used. A resident asked whether the lanes would be cleared of snow in winter, and the public works director said a smaller plow would be bought for that purpose. Another resident raised concerns about delivery trucks double parking in the lanes, and the police chief said enforcement would focus on education for the first six months, followed by fines. The school district supported the plan because two schools sit near Main Street and many students already ride bikes there despite the heavy traffic. The council agreed to hold one more public hearing next month, publish the full traffic study online, and vote on the plan in early summer. Members also asked staff to look into adding more bike racks downtown, extending the lanes to the train station in a second phase, and running a temporary pilot with removable barriers so people can try the layout before it becomes permanent. The meeting ended with a short update on the library renovation, which is two weeks behind schedule because of a delayed shipment of windows, and on the summer concert series, which will move to the riverside park this year.
